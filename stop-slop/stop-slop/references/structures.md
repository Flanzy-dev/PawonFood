# Structures to Avoid

## Binary Contrasts

Avoid repeated patterns such as "Not X, but Y," "X is not the problem; Y is," and "The answer is not X; it is Y" when they manufacture drama. State Y directly unless the distinction between X and Y carries necessary meaning.

## Negative Listing

Do not list several things a subject is not before revealing what it is. State the positive claim first.

## Dramatic Fragmentation

Avoid stacked fragments, artificial revelations, and repeated one-line emphasis. Use complete sentences unless a fragment serves the writer's established voice.

## Rhetorical Setups

Cut phrases such as "What if I told you," "Here's what I mean," and "Think about it" when they merely announce the next claim.

## False Agency

Name the human actor when an inanimate subject receives a human action. For example, replace "the decision emerged" with the person or group that decided. Keep conventional technical shorthand when no human actor exists or when the domain uses it precisely.

## Passive Voice

Prefer active voice when passive construction hides responsibility. Retain passive voice when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, already clear, or less important than the method or result.

## Sentence Openings

Vary repeated openings. Do not ban question words categorically; restructure them when they become a noticeable crutch.

## Rhythm

- Vary list length and sentence structure.
- Avoid repeatedly answering rhetorical questions immediately.
- Vary paragraph endings.
- Replace decorative em dashes with commas, parentheses, or periods.
- Avoid stacks of short, punchy fragments.

## Overgeneralization

Check words such as "every," "always," "never," and "nobody." Replace them when the evidence supports a narrower claim.
