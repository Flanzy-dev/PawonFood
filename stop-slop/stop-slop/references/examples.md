# Before and After Examples

## Example 1: Throat-Clearing and Binary Contrast

**Before:**
> "Here's the thing: building products is hard. Not because the technology is complex. Because people are complex. Let that sink in."

**After:**
> "Building products is hard because teams must coordinate people with different goals and assumptions."

**Changes:** Removed the opener, binary contrast, fragments, and emphasis crutch. Added the specific source of difficulty.

## Example 2: Filler and Unnecessary Reassurance

**Before:**
> "It turns out that most teams struggle with alignment. The uncomfortable truth is that nobody wants to admit they're confused. And that's okay."

**After:**
> "Teams often struggle with alignment because members hesitate to admit confusion."

**Changes:** Removed throat-clearing, unsupported absolutes, and permission-granting language while preserving the causal claim.

## Example 3: Business Jargon

**Before:**
> "In today's fast-paced landscape, we need to lean into discomfort and navigate uncertainty with clarity. This matters because your competition isn't waiting."

**After:**
> "Test uncertain assumptions early because competitors may reach customers first."

**Changes:** Replaced jargon and vague urgency with a concrete action and consequence.

## Example 4: Dramatic Fragmentation

**Before:**
> "Speed. Quality. Cost. You can only pick two. That's it. That's the tradeoff."

**After:**
> "The project team must balance speed, quality, and cost under its available resources."

**Changes:** Replaced fragments and an unsupported rule with a qualified statement.

## Example 5: Rhetorical Setup

**Before:**
> "What if I told you that the best teams don't optimize for productivity? Here's what I mean: they optimize for learning. Think about it."

**After:**
> "Strong teams prioritize learning when uncertainty makes short-term productivity measures unreliable."

**Changes:** Removed the rhetorical setup and binary contrast, then qualified the claim.

## Example 6: Academic Prose

**Before:**
> "It is important to note that the results clearly show that the proposed architecture is actually very effective in handling a large number of transactions."

**After:**
> "The proposed architecture processed 1,000 transactions without failed rows, although average confirmation latency reached 1,099.009 seconds."

**Changes:** Replaced vague evaluation and intensifiers with measured evidence and a relevant limitation.
