**Core concept:**
PawonFood connects households, boarding-house residents, food and beverage businesses, and community organizations that have surplus edible food with nearby people who need it.

Food can be shared for free, offered at an affordable price, or offered with room for price negotiation.

The main goal is to rescue edible surplus food before it becomes waste. PawonFood is a community food-rescue platform, not merely a discount-food marketplace.

**Primary audiences:** Hackathon judges, university communities, technology practitioners, sustainability advocates, and potential collaborators.

### The problem PawonFood addresses

According to Bappenas's 2021 food loss and waste study, Indonesia generated an estimated 23–48 million tonnes of food loss and waste annually over the 2000–2019 period.

At the community level:

- Households, boarding houses, warung, and food businesses sometimes have edible surplus food that goes to waste.
- Students and boarding-house residents need accessible, affordable meals.
- Household kitchens are an important source of surplus food but are not adequately served by existing food-rescue approaches.
- Sharing food between strangers introduces concerns about trust, food condition, collection deadlines, and reliable handovers.

Use national statistics only if they strengthen the narrative and can be communicated accurately. Do not overload the pitch with statistics.

### PawonFood's main solution

PawonFood connects people who have surplus food to people nearby who can collect it.

The core user journey is:

1. A household or food business has surplus edible food.
2. The provider photographs the food through the app's camera flow.
3. An on-device YOLO model checks the food image.
4. The food listing becomes discoverable through the application's feed or map.
5. Nearby receivers find the listing and can negotiate or reserve the food.
6. The receiver collects the food at the pickup point.
7. The receiver scans the provider's QR code to confirm collection.
8. A successful pickup contributes to the platform's intended food-rescue and community-impact metrics.

The key differentiator is its focus on household-level, hyperlocal food rescue, including food from everyday kitchens, ibu kos, and local warung.

### Important product details

Use these features where relevant to the pitch:

- Food discovery within a walking-distance, approximately 1 km radius.
- A camera-based food-listing flow.
- YOLO-based image validation.
- Free food, fixed-price food, and price negotiation.
- Reservation and pickup deadlines.
- QR-confirmed collection.
- Provider ratings and intended impact tracking.
- A community-centered approach that avoids labeling receivers as needy or stigmatizing people who collect free food.

The MVP does not include real in-app payment processing. Paid food is paid for in cash at pickup. QRIS payment is a simulated, post-MVP flow.

The current app has substantial UI screens and demonstrates the main user journey, but certain features use mock data and simulated interactions rather than a production backend. The map and listings use mock or hardcoded data, and QR scanning is simulated in the in-memory demo. The YOLO validator currently supports two classes; sealed food is selected manually.

Do not falsely describe the product as a fully deployed, production-ready platform.

The AI image validator helps identify eligible food images; it does not certify food safety or guarantee that food is safe to eat.
